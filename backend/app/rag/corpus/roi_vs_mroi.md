---
title: ROI versus Marginal ROI
topic: roi_mroi

Return on investment in a marketing mix model is usually reported as a point
estimate with a credible interval — for example an ROI of 3.2 meaning the model
attributes $3.20 of outcome per dollar spent. This is an average across the
observed spend. It answers "how productive was this channel overall?" but it
does not answer "should I spend more?"

The question of whether to spend more is governed by marginal ROI: the return on
the next incremental dollar. Because of saturation, marginal ROI is almost
always lower than average ROI, and it declines as spend rises. The right way to
allocate budget is to equalize marginal ROI across channels — move money from
channels with low marginal returns to channels with high marginal returns until
the marginal returns are balanced. Allocating by average ROI systematically
over-invests in already-saturated channels.

Interpreting ROI also requires attention to uncertainty. A point ROI of 3.2 with
a 95% credible interval of 2.8 to 3.6 is a confident estimate. The same point
estimate with an interval of 0.5 to 6.0 is nearly uninformative — the data can
not distinguish a losing channel from a highly profitable one. Ranking channels
by point ROI while ignoring overlapping intervals produces false precision.

When comparing two channels, check whether their credible intervals overlap. If
they overlap substantially, the model cannot confidently say one outperforms the
other, and any ranking between them should be presented as provisional.
